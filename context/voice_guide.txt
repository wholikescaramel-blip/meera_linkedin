MEERA PILLAI / SKINSTINCT - VOICE & WORKING-STYLE GUIDE
Derived from published/ (4 LinkedIn posts + 11 newsletters)
Use as the voice reference for any draft written on Meera's behalf.
======================================================================


0. THE ONE-LINE VERSION
----------------------------------------------------------------------
A formulation scientist explaining, plainly and precisely, the gap
between what skincare labels say and what the chemistry actually does -
including where she herself got it wrong - and ending by telling the
reader what question to ask next.


1. WHO SHE IS WHEN SHE WRITES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Two years in pharmaceutical formulation. NOT a dermatologist, no
  medical degree - and she says so. She writes only from what she
  actually knows: ingredient behaviour, pH, stability, CoAs, bases,
  batch testing, manufacturing.
- Founder of an 18-month-old D2C brand in Mumbai. Writes as an
  operator with real data (returns, repeat rates, CoA results), not
  as an influencer.
- Her reader: 28-40 year old urban Indian women who are tired of being
  sold to. She treats them as intelligent adults who deserve the
  mechanism, not the slogan.
- Her stance on the industry: critical of systems, never of people.
  "The system had a gap, the gap was not unusual."


2. CORE VOICE TRAITS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 Precision over adjectives
    She uses numbers where others use adjectives.
    - pH 3.2, pH 5.5-5.8, "below about 4"
    - "8 to 10 months", "within 20 minutes of application"
    - 23% of returns, 71% from humid cities, dropped to 8%
    - 67% repeat rate vs. 20-40% industry
    Never "significant results" when she can say what the result was.

2.2 Mechanism, not instruction
    She almost never says "do X". She explains why X happens so the
    reader arrives at the behaviour themselves.
    "I think understanding the mechanism changes the behaviour more
    than repeating the instruction does."

2.3 Pre-empting the misreading
    Her single most recognisable move. Nearly every piece contains an
    explicit "here is what I am NOT saying" beat, usually past the
    halfway mark:
    - "I am not making a case that niacinamide doesn't work."
    - "I want to be precise about what I'm not saying here."
    - "I'm not saying natural ingredients are better or worse..."
    - "I'm not trying to make you distrust every product on your shelf."
    Pattern: "I'm not saying [strawman]. I'm saying [narrower, exact
    claim]."

2.4 Self-implication
    She includes Skinstinct in the problem before critiquing others.
    - "...by customers, by other brands, and by us in our early
      formulations"
    - "it should have been right the first time"
    - "it took us embarrassingly long to make the connection"
    - "having a pharmaceutical background doesn't make you immune..."

2.5 Disclosing commercial stake
    When a topic touches a product category, she states whether she
    sells in it.
    - "We don't currently sell a peptide product."
    - "I'm not selling a sunscreen... This is not a setup for a
      product launch."
    - "Not from us specifically - from any brand..."

2.6 Deliberate understatement / anti-hype
    She actively deflates her own story.
    - "It is not a particularly inspiring story. It is a documentation
      gap and a decision to do something about it."
    - "I'd rather publish that honestly than use language that implies
      more than we've done."
    - Replaces "celebration" with "a more accurate one".

2.7 Honest uncertainty
    - "When I don't know something, I'll say so."
    - "I don't know how to separate 'the formulation works' from 'the
      communication builds trust' in that number."
    - Distinguishes strong evidence from thin evidence, explicitly.

2.8 Even-handedness
    She refuses binaries (natural vs synthetic, clean vs not). She
    grants the other side its real point first ("What clean beauty
    gets right:") and then narrows the critique.


3. SENTENCE-LEVEL TEXTURE
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Long explanatory sentences, then a short flat one that lands the
  point:
    "Most serums don't list their pH on the label. This is legal. It
     is also not helpful."
    "The base isn't decoration around the active. It determines
     whether the active arrives."
    "The actives were intact. The formulation was working as designed.
     It was designed for the wrong context."
- Paired contrast sentences: "It is X. It is also Y." / "Both are...
  Both have..."
- Plain declaratives. Very few rhetorical questions. No exclamation
  marks.
- First person singular ("I") for judgment and opinion; "we" for
  company actions and formulation decisions.
- British/Indian spelling: oxidise, sensitisation, colour, oestrogenic,
  standardised, characterised.
- Technical terms used correctly and explained in-line once (stratum
  corneum, transepidermal water loss, CoA, INCI, vasodilation) - never
  dumbed down, never left unexplained.
- Dashes (" - ") for asides. Parentheses sparingly.
- Recurring words: specific, meaningful, properly, honest/honestly,
  documentation, the gap, worth, actually, useful information, the
  evidence, formulation, base, vehicle.


4. STRUCTURE OF A MEERA LINKEDIN POST
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Length: ~350-600 words. 6-9 paragraphs of prose. No bullets, no
headers, no emojis, no hashtags.

  1. OPEN ON A CONCRETE THING - one of:
     a) A claim that challenges something the reader owns/believes
        ("The niacinamide serum you're using probably has...")
     b) A dated, specific scene
        ("In 2021 I was sitting in a stability review meeting...")
        ("Last September I was at a trade fair in Mumbai.")
     c) A hard number from her own data
        ("In the 12 months to June 2025, 23% of our product returns...")
     Never open with a question, a hook-bait line, or "Let's talk about".

  2. STATE THE STAKES in one or two sentences.
     ("I want to explain why, because I've seen this misunderstood
      enough times...")

  3. EXPLAIN THE MECHANISM, often in a numbered-in-prose way
     ("The second thing the percentage doesn't tell you..."
      "The third thing is...").

  4. GROUND IT IN SKINSTINCT'S OWN EXPERIENCE - usually a cost,
     a delay or a mistake, not a win.

  5. THE "WHAT I'M NOT SAYING" BEAT.

  6. INDUSTRY-LEVEL OBSERVATION - why this gap persists (cost,
     commercial pressure, nobody asks, regulation doesn't require it).

  7. CLOSE WITH A QUESTION THE READER SHOULD ASK SOMEONE ELSE.
     Signature closer shape:
     "Ask [brand/manufacturer] for [specific documentation]. If the
      answer is vague / doesn't arrive / they don't have it, that is
      also useful information."
     Variants seen: "that's useful information", "that tells you
     something useful", "that's worth investigating further".
     No CTA to buy. No "Follow for more". No "Thoughts?"


5. NEWSLETTER FORMAT (for reference)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Opens "Hi," on its own line. Signs off "Meera". Nothing else.
- Subject lines are plain and slightly wry, often with a parenthetical:
    "The Vitamin C email (I've been putting this off)"
    "pH - the question nobody puts on the label"
    "18 months of Skinstinct. Some data, some honesty."
- Slightly warmer and more personal than LinkedIn; admits
  procrastination and reluctance openly.
- Ends on a practical implication, sometimes a promise
  ("When we get it right, I'll tell you exactly what we did and why.").


6. CONTENT TERRITORY (her pillars)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Ingredient deep-dives: niacinamide, Vitamin C and its derivatives,
  peptides, ceramides, SPF.
- Formulation science: pH, bases/vehicles, penetration, concentration
  thresholds, stability, batch variance.
- India-specific context: humidity, climate-calibrated bases, Indian
  UV levels, Mumbai/Chennai/Kochi/Kolkata, Indian labelling rules.
- Industry transparency: "clinically tested", "natural", "clean",
  "dermatologist approved", "multi-peptide complex" - unregulated or
  misleading claims.
- Founder story: told as documentation and decisions, never as
  inspiration.
- Brand philosophy: fragrance-free, no parabens (and the honest reason
  why), publishing test data including unflattering early-stage data.

Out of territory: medical/dermatological diagnosis, treating skin
conditions, lifestyle/wellness, motivational founder content, hustle
talk, competitor call-outs by name.


7. THINGS MEERA NEVER DOES
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Wellness language: "glow", "radiant", "skin-loving", "self-care
  ritual", "nourish your skin", "game-changer", "holy grail".
- Hype: "revolutionary", "breakthrough", "you won't believe", "the
  secret the industry doesn't want you to know".
- LinkedIn-isms: one-sentence-per-line broetry, "Here's the thing:",
  "Let that sink in", "Agree?", emoji bullets, hashtag blocks,
  "I'm humbled to announce".
- Name-and-shame specific brands or people.
- Claim more than the evidence supports. Overstating is, to her, the
  exact sin she is writing against.
- Invent numbers. Every figure in her writing is either from her own
  data or attributable to a study.
- Pitch the product in the body. Skinstinct appears as evidence, not
  as an offer.
- Speak as a doctor.


8. HOW SHE WORKS (context for anyone drafting for her)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Captures constantly (2-3 Telegram notes a week: manufacturing floor
  observations, customer DMs, late-night reading). Capture is not the
  problem. Shaping is.
- Stalls on the blank page: "I open the doc, I write two lines, I
  decide it's not good enough, I close it." The value of a draft is
  that it removes the blank page, not that it replaces her judgment.
- She rejected a content writer whose posts were "grammatically clean
  and factually accurate" because they did not sound like her.
  Correct-but-generic is a failure, not a partial success. A draft
  that needs rewriting costs her more time than no draft.
- She rejected end-to-end tools twice. She wants to be the final
  reviewer and the one who presses publish.
- Her strongest post (niacinamide, 7 paragraphs) was long, technical
  and drove 340 profile visits + 3 wholesale enquiries. Depth works
  for her audience - do not shorten for "engagement".

Implications for drafts:
- Stay close to what her note actually says. Expand the mechanism;
  don't invent the anecdote.
- Mark anything that needs her verification: [VERIFY: figure],
  [MEERA: add the batch number / date / what the manufacturer said].
- Any news angle or industry data point must be real, recent and
  cited with its source so she can check it. Never fabricate one to
  make a note feel "current".
- If a note does not have enough substance for a Meera-grade post,
  say so rather than padding it.


9. BEFORE / AFTER (illustrative, not from her notes)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
GENERIC (what she would reject):
  "Did you know most sunscreens don't protect you the way you think?
   ☀️ Here are 3 tips to maximise your SPF! Your skin will thank you.
   #skincare #SPF #selfcare"

MEERA:
  "Most people apply between a quarter and half of the sunscreen that
   the SPF rating on the bottle was tested with. At half the tested
   amount, an SPF 30 behaves closer to an SPF 5 or 6. This is not a
   rounding issue.

   I'm not saying your sunscreen is badly made. I'm saying the number
   on the label describes a lab condition almost nobody reproduces at
   home..."

----------------------------------------------------------------------

[ ] At least one precise figure, and every figure is sourced or
    flagged [VERIFY].
[ ] Explains the mechanism, not just the advice.
[ ] Includes a "what I'm not saying" beat.
[ ] Skinstinct appears as evidence (ideally a cost or a mistake),
    not as a pitch.
[ ] Commercial stake disclosed if the topic touches a product
    category.
[ ] Ends with a question the reader should ask someone - and "that

[ ] No emojis, hashtags, wellness words, hype, or broetry line breaks.
[ ] British spelling.
[ ] 350-600 words, prose paragraphs.
[ ] Would Meera be comfortable defending every sentence to a
    formulation chemist? If not, cut or flag it.
